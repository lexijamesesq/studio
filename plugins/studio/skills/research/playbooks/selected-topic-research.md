# Selected-topic research

Start from the selected candidate, its saved evidence restrictions, publishing intent and relevant lessons. Read the evidence before asking the author for context already present. Research consequential gaps rather than assembling a maximal dossier.

Develop a proposed argument, supporting and conflicting evidence, material gaps, and the next useful editorial interaction. Selection is interest in a topic, not endorsement of the proposed thesis. Invite craft and novel thought rather than asking the author to initiate from a blank page.

For this slice, saved preparation evidence must quote sources already attached to the article. If additional research is required, identify it as a gap and return what can be supported now. Adding sources to the registry and a new discovery assessment is explicit work; do not claim unaudited outside evidence as persisted support. Avoid open-ended research loops.

Return preparation matching `schemas/preparation.schema.json` including articleId, current expectedRevision and a new operationId. Call `prepare` after actual research/development; creating a preparing article alone is not preparation. Keep any novel proposal distinguishable from source-backed findings. The next interaction should help advance the argument, not perform record administration.
